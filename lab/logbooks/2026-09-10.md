## Then wrote the thing F4 said to write next

F4 closed with: *"None asserted 'no populated field ever leaves the Task without either appearing in
the brief or raising a gap'. That invariant is the actual contract ... and it should be the next
thing written."* Twenty days late.

**Measured first, before touching the renderer.** One Task with every field populated, compiled at
every (profile × phase × stakes) point. At the time, seven profiles, 126 points, 1890 field
renderings:

```
removed from the brief:  423  (22.4%)
gaps raised:               0
```

Zero. Gaps fire on absence and nothing was absent, so the linter — the part of the README that calls
itself the product — was structurally incapable of mentioning a quarter of what it was handed. The
top offender by volume is `open_decisions` at 91 of 126; the one with teeth is `escalation_triggers`
at 72, because on 2026-08-20 three of those were safety stops about pihole's port 53 and they went
out missing.

**The fix is a second channel, not more gaps.** A gap points forward — the recipient needs something
you did not supply. A drop points backward — you supplied something and the shape removed it.
Opposite directions, opposite remedies, so reporting 448 correct selections as gaps would bury the
two that are not. `Brief.drops` names the field, the dial, the value, the threshold, which side it
sits on, and why. Detection is derived from the rendered text rather than from a second copy of the
render logic, because F4 *was* two branches each assuming the other.

### Three things went wrong while building it, and all three are the point

**The detector under-counted, and only disagreement caught it.** The standalone probe said 543
removals; the detector said 417. The difference was `[0]` indexing into both a list (`acceptance` →
first criterion) and a string (`verification` → first *character*, present in every brief). Same
class as F4: one branch handling two shapes. Found by two counts disagreeing, not by reading code.

**The first version of the invariant passed the defect it was written for.** Reverted F4's fix and
the new test stayed green: it reported the lost acceptance criteria as a legitimate drop, with the
rationale *"criteria are bound inside each subtask instead"*, while the subtask list had never been
rendered. The rationale was named and never checked. An invariant that accepts a false explanation
accepts anything. Fixed by verifying the stated cause actually holds; the F4 revert now fails.

**Then it found a live one on its first honest run.** `decomposition >= 0.6` with instructions and
no subtasks skips both branches — no subtask list to render, and the instructions branch is gated on
the dial being *low*. The brief goes out with an objective, a return contract and no statement of
what to do. `subtasks_missing` fires but is advisory, so `--gate` passes it. It hits `antigravity`,
`casper`, `melchior` and `balthasar` — every profile whose decomposition is high by design. F4's
exact twin, one section further down the renderer. Fixed: the instructions render as a degraded
`Task` section, gap still raised.

**Mutation-tested the new tests** rather than trusting that they work: flipping one entry's side in
the rationale table goes red, returning `[]` from the drop detector goes red on three tests, and
reverting F4's fix goes red. A test that cannot fail is decoration.

43 tests → 52.

### A correction to F4's method note

F4 said all three of that day's defects were found by *consuming the library from outside*, never by
adding tests to it, and that self-written tests share the author's blind spots by construction. Half
right. Today's defect was found by a test — but a **property** test whose expectation came from an
independent measurement of the output, not from the author's model of the code. The distinction
worth keeping is not outside-versus-inside. It is whether the check derives from what the program
*produced* or from what its author *believes it does*.

## The larger result was not the one being looked for

Having built the measurement, ran it sideways: does phase or stakes change the brief at all?

```
144 (profile x phase x stakes) points  ->  26 distinct briefs
claude, all 18 of its points           ->   1 distinct brief
low / normal / high stakes identical   ->  31 of 48 (profile, phase) pairs
```

Resolution is continuous. The renderer reads it only through eleven `>=` thresholds. So most deltas
land inside a step and change nothing. `escalation_explicitness` is fixed regardless of phase and
stakes for 7 of 8 profiles, and the `+0.15` that high stakes adds buys an escalation section for
**none of them, in any phase** — checked exhaustively. Which is the general form of the 2026-08-20
incident: that override was not a one-off, it was the only way to get the section at all.

[F7](../findings/F7-the-dials-are-continuous-the-renderer-is-not.md), and it amends
[T1](../theory/T1-shape-as-a-function-of-recipient.md), which had committed to shape being a point
in *continuous* dimensions. At the output it is boolean; the continuity lives in the composition and
is discarded at the last step. Filed the design question as **D8** with five options and took none
of them — option 2 is a dial-value change and blocked behind D2, and option 5 (supplied stop
conditions become undroppable) changes the semantics of a dial the eval is about to measure. The
operator's call, not a measurement's.

A note in F7's favour that is worth saying out loud because it cuts the other way: this does not
damage the eval. The protocol already holds phase and stakes fixed and defines arm A as an ablation
*across the render threshold*. That was the right call for a reason nobody wrote down at the time.

## Adding one profile moved the other measurement by 0.18

Ran `bliss correlate` before and after `astra`, expecting a rounding change.

| pair | n=7 | n=8 |
|---|---|---|
| `specificity / verification_rigor` | +0.978 | **+0.754** |
| `acceptance_binding / verification_rigor` | +0.921 | **+0.796** |
| `decomposition / context_volume` | +0.773 | **+0.183** |

Mean absolute shift 0.176 across all 21 pairs. Both of the audit's merge candidates fell out of
merge range on one added profile. D6 has been refusing to merge dials on the n=7 matrix for three
weeks on the grounds that one author's habits are not a property of language models; that refusal
now has a number attached, and it would have been undone by a single new file.

The mechanism is specific rather than "correlations are noisy": `astra` is the first profile with
low `decomposition` and high `context_volume` at once. Every previous low-decomposition profile was
also low-volume, because *don't give it a checklist* and *don't give it the corpus* had happened to
travel together. The pair that measured +0.773 on that coincidence measures +0.183 now.
[F9](../findings/F9-one-profile-moved-the-whole-correlation-matrix.md).

**And the direction is convenient, which is the tell.** It favours keeping seven dials, which is
what this project would prefer. `astra`'s values were chosen today by someone who had just read F6
and F7. Said so in F9 rather than around it.

## One value chosen for the wrong kind of reason

`astra`'s `escalation_explicitness` is 0.65, and it is 0.65 partly because 0.55 would have put it
below the render threshold and silently dropped the scope boundaries the routing policy requires in
its handoff. That is a dial value set to dodge a finding rather than to describe a recipient —
exactly the unmeasured tuning D2 exists to prevent. The justification from the document is real
(*"explicit scope boundaries"* is a stated requirement), so it stays, with the reason written into
the profile file where the next person will see it. It is still the weakest number in the set.
